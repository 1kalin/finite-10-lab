## Market gaps

1. **Evidence is not a badge.** Directories sell enhanced or verified placement, while walls sell position. A credible flagship can publish separately labeled evidence status, last-tested date, methodology, sponsor status, and correction history.
2. **Discovery must exist below the canvas.** Competitor walls are visually finite but shallow. The defensible surface is useful agent profiles, use cases, integrations, alternatives, comparisons, and repeatable benchmark results; the Founding 100 wall is a brand layer, not the organic-acquisition engine.
3. **Paid placement must not sell rank.** Use fixed, clearly labeled Founding 100 inventory and `rel="sponsored"`; keep category/search ordering independent and explain it.
4. **Destination health is a product feature.** Validate links at submission, recheck them, show last-checked state, and expire/remove unsafe or dead destinations with an audit trail.
5. **Avoid permanent-service promises.** A placement licence with published minimum service terms, edit/takedown rules, and refund/cancellation policy is more truthful than “own forever.”
6. **Publish buyer outcomes only when measured.** Track qualified profile views and outbound clicks, but never imply traffic, leads, customers, or revenue before observed data exists.

## Implication for flagship selection

Agent Wall has the clearest path only when reframed as a verified agent discovery/comparison product with a finite, labeled Founding 100 sponsorship layer. A canvas-only product is falsified by the live substitutes above: several already offer cheaper or more novel pixels, while at least one plainly warns buyers not to expect traffic.
